Four Sight: How OsamaSon is Reshaping the Rap Underground

OsamaSon is among the younger rappers challenging rap’s conventions. We caught up with the South Carolina artist after the release of Jump Out.

Artist profile · Underground Rap Community

In a Complex interview, OsamaSon describes a recording routine built around privacy and comfort: dim lights, familiar snacks, pizza and horror films. He says the quiet, low-lit setup helps him settle in for long recording sessions.

That atmosphere offers one way into the music. Tracks such as “ik what you did last summer” pair uneasy moods with blown-out, dissonant production, a sound that has become a recognizable part of his work.

From Goose Creek to the underground

OsamaSon, 21, grew up in Goose Creek, South Carolina. In a short time, he has emerged as a notable voice in the noisy, rage-influenced underground, drawing comparisons to the scene shaped in part by Playboi Carti and the SoundCloud generation.

A wider sound on Jump Out

The project follows Flex Musix and features production from ok. It keeps the rough edges of OsamaSon’s earlier sound while giving the tracks more space and a fuller mix. In the interview, he describes wanting listeners to feel the lyrics, melodies and bass together rather than having the low end dominate everything.

That growth comes alongside a close but impatient audience. The interview discusses extensive leaks of unreleased music, including one reported batch of hundreds of tracks. The leaks have been frustrating for the rapper, even as they point to strong demand for what he makes.
