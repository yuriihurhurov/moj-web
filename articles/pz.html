What to Know About Pz’, the Model-Turned-Rapper Making Noise in Atlanta’s Underground Scene

Could the ØWAY member be rap’s next big style star?

Artist profile · Underground Rap Community

Rappers often enter fashion through clothing labels or by becoming style icons. Pz’ took a less familiar route: from modeling into rap. The Atlanta-born artist and member of ØWAY is drawing notice in the underground with fashion-conscious lyrics and a runway-ready image. His look also places him among Atlanta’s most stylish rappers.

An early interest in fashion

Pz’, whose name is Payasu Njie, first entered the industry as a model after being discovered in New York. Fashion was already familiar at home: his mother had modeled, according to Perfect Magazine. Growing up in Atlanta, he stayed connected to his Gambian and Senegalese roots while developing an interest in clothing and style.

Working in luxury retail in Buckhead introduced him to another side of the fashion business. A New York trip in 2022 became a turning point when he was scouted; the following year, he signed with Wilhelmina Models. Pz’ has since brought that experience into his music and public image.

His path connects two creative worlds: the visual language of fashion and Atlanta’s underground rap scene. More reporting on his music and ØWAY will help tell how that transition develops.

Pz’s Modeling Career

One of Pz’ early high-profile fashion appearances was connected to Nigerian-born designer Mowalola. The label’s Spring/Summer 2025 “Dirty Pop” collection paired hyperpop references with tactile materials such as fur and latex. Pz’ later appeared in a Mowalola footwear campaign, extending a creative relationship that also included his July 2025 track “Mowa Swag.”

In 2025, Pz’ was also featured in Hedi Slimane’s “New London” portfolio for The Face, a black-and-white series photographed in East London. The slim silhouette associated with Slimane’s styling has become part of Pz’s own look, echoed in his track “Hedis Bussin.”

In June 2026, Pz’ walked in Pharrell’s Louis Vuitton Men’s Spring/Summer 2027 show. He was also featured in the Denim Tears x Billionaire Boys Club collection, continuing to move between Atlanta rap and fashion campaigns.

The ØWAY Cypher

The ØWAY cypher became a breakthrough moment for Pz’. Over the group’s loud, otherworldly production, his smoother delivery and monochrome-red styling made him stand out. The response helped push ØWAY into a wider spotlight, with Young Thug showing support for co-founders Tezzus and diamond* by taking them on tour.

Another major look came from Playboi Carti, who reposted Pz’s breakout track “Hedis Bussin.” The accompanying #freepz hashtag circulated during a period of reported legal uncertainty; it also became a call for wider recognition of Pz’s music. In May 2026, Pz’ appeared onstage with Carti at Rolling Loud, further drawing attention to his connection with YVL. He remains part of ØWAY while building a distinct public image of his own.

What’s Next?

In an interview with Cash Cow Music, ØWAY co-founder Tezzus framed the collective as a commitment to artistic freedom and resistance to industry expectations. Pz’ reflects that approach through work that brings his multicultural background, fashion and music together. As ØWAY grows, he continues to develop his own identity within the group.

“No Turning Back”

In summer 2026, Pz’ released his first full-length project, No Turning Back. The album pairs a cleaner, more polished finish with the edge and attitude associated with ØWAY. On “Never Gon’ Home,” he looks back on his early rise and the persistence that carried him forward.

“Fête Feeling” blends Atlanta underground rap with African-influenced instrumentation; its title draws on the French word for a party or celebration. Pz’ also appeared on “Gotta Love Us,” the opening track of Slime Language 3.
